Micro-Mechanics of Lithium Electro-Chemo-Mechanical Dendrite Percolation

Garnet-type solid electrolytes ($\text{Li}_7\text{La}_3\text{Zr}_2\text{O}_{12}$, LLZO) boast superior ionic conductivity ($\sim 1\,\text{mS/cm}$) and wide electrochemical stability windows ($0\text{--}6\,\text{V}$). However, contrary to classical Monroe-Newman theory, rigid ceramics do not mechanically suppress dendrites: spatial current heterogeneity drives intergranular lithium metal wedge growth, emitting characteristic high-frequency acoustic waves prior to electrical shorting.

Section 01 // Fracture Mechanics

Grain-Boundary Overpotential & Wedge Crack Extension

Lithium deposition inside grain boundary defect pores produces extreme local hydrostatic pressure $P_{\text{hyd}}$. When the local stress intensity factor $K_I$ matches or exceeds the critical ceramic fracture toughness $K_{Ic}$ of polycrystalline LLZO ($\sim 0.9\text{--}1.2\,\text{MPa}\cdot\text{m}^{1/2}$), Griffith micro-cracking propagates instantaneously:

$$K_I = 2 P_{\text{hyd}} \sqrt{\frac{c}{\pi}} + \sigma_{\text{applied}} \ge K_{Ic} = \sqrt{2 E \gamma_{\text{gb}}}$$

Here $c$ is flaw radius, $E$ is Young's modulus ($\sim 150\,\text{GPa}$), and $\gamma_{\text{gb}}$ is grain-boundary surface energy. The elastic strain energy release $\Delta U_e$ is channeled directly into transient acoustic wave acceleration $a(t)$:

$$a(t) = \frac{1}{\rho v_s} \frac{\partial^2}{\partial t^2} \left[ \int_{\Omega} \sigma_{ij}(x,t) d\Omega \right] \propto \frac{\Delta U_e}{\tau_{\text{rise}}}$$
Key Signature: 0.8–5.2 MHz shear/longitudinal wave emission with sub-microsecond acoustic rise times.
Section 02 // Electrochemical Kinetics

Electro-Chemo-Mechanical Butler-Volmer Kinetics

At the tip of a propagating lithium filament, the overpotential $\eta$ is amplified by local electrical field convergence and stress-dependent electrochemical potential modification:

$$J_{\text{tip}} = J_0 \left[ \exp\left( \frac{\alpha F (\eta - \Omega \Delta \sigma)}{RT} \right) - \exp\left( -\frac{(1-\alpha) F (\eta - \Omega \Delta \sigma)}{RT} \right) \right]$$

$\Omega$ represents the molar volume of lithium metal ($1.3 \times 10^{-5}\,\text{m}^3/\text{mol}$). As the conductive metallic filament breaches the separator, the effective electrolyte thickness $L_{\text{eff}} = L_0 - z_{\text{dendrite}}(t)$ collapses, inducing a nonlinear resistance drop:

$$R(t) = R_{\text{bulk}} \left( 1 - \frac{z_{\text{dendrite}}(t)}{L_0} \right) + R_{\text{contact}} + \sum \Delta R_{\text{gb}}$$
Coupling: $R(t)$ declines smoothly while $a(t)$ exhibits stochastic, discrete bursts coincident with grain facet splits.
Section 03 // State Estimation Algorithm

High-Speed Dual-Kalman Filter (EKF-Observer)

Standard battery management systems (BMS) cannot detect micro-dendrites because terminal voltage variations are buried in background polarization noise. The SolidElectrolyte algorithm employs a dual-state extended observer:

$$\hat{\mathbf{x}}_{k} = \mathbf{f}(\hat{\mathbf{x}}_{k-1}, u_{k-1}) + \mathbf{K}_k \left[ \mathbf{y}_k - \mathbf{h}(\hat{\mathbf{x}}_k) \right], \quad \mathbf{y} = \begin{bmatrix} a_{\text{RMS}} \\ R_{\text{bulk}} \end{bmatrix}$$

The crack damage parameter $D \in [0, 1]$ is estimated through cross-correlation metric $\chi_{\text{dendrite}}$:

$$\chi_{\text{dendrite}} = \int_{t-\tau}^{t} \left( \frac{a(\tau)}{\sigma_a} \right) \cdot \left| \frac{1}{R(\tau)} \frac{dR(\tau)}{d\tau} \right| d\tau \ge \Theta_{\text{critical}}$$
Safety Intervention: Autonomous cutoff triggered if $\chi_{\text{dendrite}} > 0.85$, $350\,\text{ms}$ before electrical dead-short.
Section 04 // Transducer Instrumentation

PZT Ring Sensor & Cryogenic Silicon Integration

For lunar operational survival ($-180^\circ\text{C}$ to $+120^\circ\text{C}$), standard commercial piezo transducers fail due to Curie point drift and epoxy embrittlement. The system implements thin-film Gallium Orthophosphate ($\text{GaPO}_4$) and lead zirconate titanate (PZ27) arrays sputtering-bonded directly to the ceramic electrolyte perimeter:

$$V_{\text{out}}(t) = g_{33} \cdot \sigma_z(t) \cdot h_{\text{sensor}} = \frac{d_{33}}{\varepsilon_{33}^T} \cdot \left[ \rho v_p \int a(t) dt \right] \cdot h$$

Combined with multi-frequency lock-in AC excitation ($10\,\text{kHz}\text{--}500\,\text{kHz}$) through current collector leads, bulk resistance $R$ and acoustic acceleration $a$ are monitored concurrently on a single ASIC front-end.
